## 1 · *"without somenoe to talk to we can t do anything but view it. it should always be interactive."*

### The finding, and he is exactly right `[CODE]`

`markOf` returns a kind only when one of three clauses fires — `pendingAt(n)!==undefined` (work),
`roomPending(n)` (a room host with a question), `n.doc` (something to read). Meridian declares two of
the three. **When nobody in the city wants anything, `markOf` returns `null` for every person,
`planMarks()` returns `[]`, and every surface that reads it goes quiet at once:**

- `mapPick` iterates `planMarks()`. An empty list means **a tap can never hit anything**, anywhere on
  the paper. It returns `false` and nothing happens.
- `mapLegend` sets `box.hidden` and draws no key.
- `mapNoteSet` falls through to `pl.none`.

What is left is one control: `mapAnyBtn` → `mapRandomDest`, which picks a **random** nameable person
anywhere in the city. It was the right answer to the question it was built for (*"we should also be
able to point to a random destnation"*) and it is not an answer to this one. **A button that chooses
for you is the opposite of *"i can choose my destination"***, which is his own sentence from
2026-09-15 and the reason the picking exists at all.

So the plan is interactive exactly as long as the city has homework for you, and becomes a picture
the moment you finish it. That is backwards: the map is *most* useful when there is no ❗ to follow.

### The recommendation — a PLACE is not a MARK, and only one of them is about quests

Split the two nouns that are currently one.

- **`planMarks()` stays exactly what it is** — who is waiting, painted by kind, `MAPMARK`'s order.
  Nothing about the mark work from 2026-09-14/16 is given up.
- **`planPlaces()` is new and is not about quests at all** — every addressable thing on the paper:
  each panel's portal tiles (`portalsOf` already lists them), each world's anchor (`planPlace`
  already computes it), each `transit` tile, and the park, which `MAPDOT` names because nothing can
  derive it. A place exists whether or not anybody is standing in it.
- **`mapPick` reads places.** Marks become an overlay on top of them.

That is one seam, and it is the same shape as every other seam in this engine: the **rule** is that
a place on the paper can be chosen (engine); the **choice** is which glyphs are places, which the
pack already declares in `DOORS`, `TILES[g].kind` and `MAPDOT`.

### The trap, named before it is built

Places are **denser than marks**, and the research already wrote the rule for this:
`docs/research/2026-09-14-map-and-questionnaires.md` **M6**, revised against WCAG 2.5.8 —
*a target is a place, not a tile: each mark ≥ 24×24 CSS px; spacing between marks may be tighter,
because the standard exempts map pins for position, not for size.* A tile is **9.39 CSS px** at
390 px wide. `mapPick`'s `bd>9` is a three-tile radius, which clears the floor for marks and will
be **ambiguous between two doors two tiles apart** — which La Espiga and Velázquez nearly are.

The answer is not a smaller target. It is the same control §3 needs: **one target, a two-line
chooser.** Which is why §3 and §1 should be built together or not at all.

### Guard, red first

With **every quest in the game answered** — `done` full, `planMarks()` empty, proven empty in the
same test — **a tap on a door on the plan still sets a destination.** Red today: it returns `false`.

Not *"places were found"*. A count whose noun is a tile on a map is `docs/REGRESSION.md` §3's
commonest shape and this project has shipped it twice. Ask the behaviour: tap, then read `mapDest`.

## 2 · *"we can set a destination to remember where we meant to go"*

### The finding `[CODE]`

`mapDest` is a module-level `let`. It is **not in `save()`** — the `st` object it builds carries
nineteen fields and no destination. `destCheck` nulls it on arrival by design.

So: reload the page and it is gone. Close the tab on the way somewhere and it is gone. Get the
trolley, put the phone down, come back — gone. **Every one of those is the exact moment a person
would most want it**, because a destination that survives nothing is a destination you only have
while you are already walking to it.

### The rule it looks like it collides with, and why it does not — `docs/ARCH-LOG.md` A3

The comment above `mapDest` says it: *"never saved. A destination is what you are doing right now; a
saved one is the list A3 bans, wearing a compass."*

**A3 does not say that.** A3's own three lines are:

> A record of the **past** — always fine. A record of the **present** — who is waiting right now —
> fine. A record of the **future** — *a list of what you have not done yet* — is the banned thing.

**One destination that you set yourself is not a list.** It is not generated by the game, it is not
ranked, it does not grow, and it disappears the moment you arrive. The comment overreached, and the
owner has now drawn the line himself. **A3 gets a dated line saying so.**

The boundary that keeps it honest, and it should be written into A3 in the same edit: **one pin,
yours, saved. The day a second pin is wanted, that is a new decision and not an extension of this
one** — because the [WEB] framing (§8) is that persistence is precisely what buys *planning*, and a
planning surface with several pins on it is the banned list with a nicer name.

### The recommendation, and the one trap in it

`save()` carries `d:{w,x,y,who}` and **nothing else**.

`mapDest` also holds `gx,gy` — **paper** coordinates — and those are *derived*, not facts. Saving
them is `docs/BEAUTIFY.md`'s *"two coordinate systems, one field"* lesson happening a second time:
edit `TOWNPLAN`, reload, and a saved `gy` draws its ring on the wrong panel with nothing failing.
**Recompute `gx,gy` from `planPlace` on load, every time.**

It crosses `sanitizeSave`, which is the project's trust boundary (`docs/BOUNDARY.md`, Zeni's
register — Trolley Pass links arrive through this same door). So: `w` must be a key of `WORLDS`,
`x`/`y` clamp to that world's `W`/`H`, `who` must name a person the pack actually declared, and
anything else drops. A destination is now something that can arrive from outside.

### Guard, red first

Two reds, and the second is the one that matters:

1. Set a destination, save, reload, and the **same `w,x,y`** comes back.
2. Set a destination, save, **move that world's panel in `TOWNPLAN`**, reload — and the ring is
   still on the right building. That is the one a saved `gx,gy` fails and a recomputed one passes.

## 3 · *"we should have it expanded for the second floors"*

### The finding, and it is worse than "they are missing" `[CODE]`

There are exactly **two** upstairs worlds in Meridian: `f2` (Floor 2, behind `hq`, up the one flight
in the city that climbs — `PORTALS.hq["▲"]`, `mark:"up"`) and `no` (Nolasco, up five treads from the
`$` door on Calle Principal).

`planPlace`'s third clause, written yesterday, says: *a world behind a world that is itself only a
dot **shares its address***. That is right about the city — the second floor of the office **is** the
office — and it has a consequence nobody looked at:

- `hq` resolves to `st`(14,1). `f2` resolves to **hq's exact `gx,gy`**.
- `planMarks`' `at[key]` dedupe then folds them into **one anchor**, carrying **one** mark and
  `ws:["hq","f2"]`.
- **Floor 2 therefore cannot be chosen on the plan at all.** Whichever of the two wins `MAPMARK`'s
  order is what a tap gives you. `PL.upstairs:"f2"` exists only as a `⇧` in the map note, and
  `TOWNLBL` writes `MERIDIAN HQ  (⇧ FLOOR 2)` and `NOTARIO ⇧` **by hand** — a label doing a
  structure's job, which is always the tell.
- `no` does get its own paper address (`st` is a drawn panel, so the `$` door at 17,4 is a real
  offset) and is then drawn **as if it were at street level**.

### Two shapes, costed, one recommended

**(a) A floor selector.** The standard pattern `[WEB]`: a small vertical stack in a corner, active
floor rendered, the others as semi-transparent footprints, buttons capped at about five. It is the
right control for a building with many floors and the **wrong** control here — Meridian has two
upstairs rooms in a fifteen-world city, and a `1 | 2` toggle for the whole city is a control that is
wrong on every tile but two. **Recommend against.**

**(b) An address carries a level, and a place with something above it opens a chooser.**
`planPlace` already returns `via` — it knows which door it came through — and a portal that declares
`mark:"up"` is the engine saying *this is a LEVEL, not a neighbour*. So a place whose derivation
crossed such a portal draws a **corner fold** (the paper's own idiom, and the plan is already
folded paper), and tapping it opens a **two-line chooser**: *Meridian HQ · Floor 2*.

**Recommend (b)**, and the reason is that it is not a new control at all — **it is the control M6
already demanded for a shared anchor**, which Rosa and Tavo both filed in crew run 8. One build
closes the second-floor gap, the shared-anchor fault, and §1's density problem. And it makes
`TOWNLBL`'s two hand-written `⇧` redundant, which is how you know the structure was missing rather
than the label.

### Guard, red first

Put somebody who is waiting in **hq** and somebody who is waiting in **f2** at the same time, and
insist both are separately reachable from the plan. Red today: one anchor, one mark, one of the two
unreachable.

## 4 · *"the label about doors as gold is wrong for the calle dos"*

He is right, and the measurement is worse than the complaint. **Three defects in one line of code.**

### (a) A pack cannot say it `[CODE]`

`drawTown` draws it hardcoded, with an `es?` ternary:

```
g2.fillText(es?"puertas y escaleras en dorado · ◉ estás aquí":"doors & stairs in gold · ◉ you are here", …)
```

A second world gets **Meridian's sentence, in Meridian's two languages**, under its own map. Same
class as the PAPER seam gap (`docs/ARCH-LOG.md` A15 — *a pack cannot design its own paper*), and
that one was found the same way: by a world that was not Meridian needing to say something else.

### (b) It is drawn once, centred under the WHOLE paper

At `mc.width/2, w.H*s+12`. `w.H` is the height of **all** panels stacked, so since `TOWNPLAN` gave
the paper a second panel the caption has been sitting under Calle Dos as well as Calle Principal —
one sentence claiming to describe two streets that are painted differently.

### (c) And on Calle Dos it is false, measurably — nobody had checked `[CODE]`

Gold is `#E0B45C`. It is carried by `E L O 2` (`BASECOL`) and `M % 1` (`MAPCOL`).

| Glyph | What it is | What the plan actually paints |
|---|---|---|
| `2` | the link between the two streets | `#E0B45C` — **gold** |
| `@` | **La Espiga's door** | *no entry in `MAPCOL` or `BASECOL`* → `planTile`'s last branch: the generic `#A79B86` box |
| `*` | **Velázquez's door** | same — the generic `#A79B86` box |
| `$` | **Nolasco's door** | same — the generic `#A79B86` box |
| `⌂` | the homes on Calle Dos | `#8A5A2B` brown |

**On the Calle Dos panel the only gold tile is the way back to Calle Principal.** Both shop doors
fall into the branch `planTile`'s own comment calls *"a thing nobody ever thought about on this
map"*. And `MAPCOL` has been confessing it in a code comment since the day it was written:
*"the legend says 'doors & stairs in gold' and the plan painted them grey."*

**"Stairs" is worse, and it is wrong on BOTH streets.** `1` is gold — that is the old flight. The
flight the city actually uses is `⊓ ≡ ▲ ▼`, and: `▲` is `#241F2E` (near-black), `▼` is `#C6BEAA`
(pale stone), `≡` has no colour at all and is another grey box. **Three of the four are not gold,
and the two that are actually doors — `▲` and `▼`, the head and the foot of every flight — are the
two furthest from it.**

### The recommendation — the caption stops asserting and starts reporting

Three steps, and each is worth shipping alone:

1. **`plan.caption` becomes a pack string**, per panel where a pack wants one, and the engine's own
   claim is deleted. `◉ you are here` is true everywhere and can stay in the engine. *(minutes)*
2. **Give Calle Dos's doors their colours back** — `@`, `*`, `$` into `MAPCOL`, each in its own
   business's hue the way `DOORLOOK` already does in the street (wheat, teal, burgundy). Content
   only, no engine change. *(minutes)*
3. **Derive the claim.** The engine already builds `col={...BASECOL,...MAPCOL}` and `planTile`
   already knows what it painted. Walk the panel, collect the colours used by tiles whose glyph is
   in `DOORSET` or whose `TILES[g].kind==="stair"`, and **if they are not one colour the caption
   does not claim they are.** A caption that can only say what was painted cannot go stale.
   *(minutes, once §1's place walk exists)*

### Guard, red first

**A caption may not name a colour the panel under it did not paint.** Walk each panel, collect the
door and stair colours actually used, fail if the caption claims gold and the panel has none. Red
today, on the Calle Dos panel, in both languages. And a second, cruder red that cannot break the
same way: a pack that declares **no** `plan.caption` gets **no claim** — not Meridian's.

## 5 · *"when you select some destination or person to chat with, can we do like a small light path for it"*

### The decision it collides with, reported and not absorbed

`docs/meetings/2026-09-14-el-mapa.md` §7.3 signs the opposite, and the engine repeats it in a comment
above `BEARS`: *"a DIRECTION, never a lit path — Elden Ring's Guidance of Grace is the shape of it."*

**The case for that decision is real** `[WEB]`. Fable II's always-on golden trail drew exactly the
critique the repo was avoiding — players reporting they *"never got a sense of location"* because
the trail simply led them to the next thing. Dead Space's line was the one players preferred, and
the named reason was that it was **on demand**, not always on.

**And both halves of that finding are already inside his sentence.** *"when you select"* — you asked
for it. *"a small light path"* — he said small. He did not ask for Fable's trail; he asked for Dead
Space's line, in his own words, without having read either.

### The recommendation, which gives up nothing

**Draw the path on the PAPER, not in the street.**

A line on a map is what maps are for. A line painted on the ground is what Fable did. §7.3's whole
argument is about the *world* teaching you where you are, and it is untouched: **the street keeps the
bearing and only the bearing.** The plan — a thing you deliberately open, look at, and close — is
where a route has always belonged.

### And it pays for itself twice: the route IS the portal graph, drawn

`planPlace` already walks the doors recursively to answer *where is this world*. Run the same walk
**from where you are standing to what you picked**, and the segments it returns are the route:
`st` → the `$` door → `no`.

That is precisely what the stairs plan asked for — *"the map also can help us map our world through
portals to keep track of this"* (`docs/plans/2026-09-17-stairs-and-drawing.md`) — and it turns a
wrong portal from something a guard can assert into something **you can see**. A door that claims to
lead somewhere it does not draws a route that visibly goes the wrong way.

### Shape and cost

A dashed warm line under the marks, one segment per hop, static; a soft glow on the **final** segment
only, so the thing that is lit is the arrival and not the walk. `prefers-reduced-motion` kills any
travel animation; the line itself never moves. *(a sitting, once §1 exists)*

**The trap, named now:** a route across two panels crosses the paper *between* them, where there is
no street. **Draw that hop as a break, not a line** — which is exactly what a pocket map does when a
route leaves the page.

### Guard, red first

The route from `st` to `no` must be the doors the engine would actually walk — plant a wrong portal
and the drawn route must change. And: **no segment may be drawn across the gap between two panels.**

## 11 · The guard fault to avoid, because this register keeps writing it

Every red above is phrased as a **behaviour**, never a count. *"A route was drawn"*, *"three colours
were used"*, *"places were found"* — a count whose noun is a mark on a tile is `docs/REGRESSION.md`
§3's commonest shape, and `docs/ARCH-LOG.md` A17 has the freshest example: a cache ceiling checked
against **the code's own constant**, which passed with the cache switched off.

Ask the cruder question that cannot break the same way. Read the pixels. Walk the whole city. And per
2026-09-17's `COUNT-ONLY:` rule, **print the count either way** — a guard that measured nothing is
still a guard that measured nothing.
